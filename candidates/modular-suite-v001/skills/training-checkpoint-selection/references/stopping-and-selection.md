# Continuing, stopping and choosing an artifact

Use for STOP, a tentative final interval, checkpoint selection or a recovery exit.
The run contract determines allowed actions, eligibility and hard safety limits.

## Continue only for a useful opportunity

Explain what another interval could improve or distinguish at its actual schedule
and cost. A recent intervention may need a response, a lull may precede recovery,
and reducing an update size can change observation time. Use these possibilities
when supported, not as permanent reasons to run to the cap. Do not impose a
universal patience or require perfect map stability.

When evidence supports stopping because useful progress is poorly supported,
say that. When it supports measured harm, name the actual quality or safety
measurement and population. Unlabeled redistribution alone cannot establish
task harm or prove later recovery impossible. A source safety exit has its own
contractual role; a budget exit is not convergence.

Do not continue solely to obtain a missing ideal metric. If no informative action
remains, stopping with uncertainty is legitimate. Conversely, inspect whether a
cheap, permitted contrast could resolve an uncertainty that really changes the
decision rather than automatically stopping after a failed scalar.

## Select from what is truly available

First check the allowed set, artifact identities, missing diagnostics and failure
states. A compact role/phase memory is an attention aid, not an eligibility filter.
Compare the strongest alternative, including an earlier useful phase or recovery
state where relevant. Never use future outcomes to justify a historical turn.

Highest source score, latest epoch, low entropy, temporal overlap and a compact
map are not universal quality rankers. Separate measured target quality, proxy
behavior and safety. If these cannot rank the candidates, state the ambiguity
and follow the contract's supported conservative choice. Do not fabricate a
numeric composite score to make the ambiguity disappear.

A fallback mode may leave the artifact choice unspecified. Distinguish an
explicit contract requirement from your own provisional selection criterion.
Explain why that criterion is useful here and compare the strongest alternative;
being eligible and noncollapsed alone does not favor the earliest or latest
artifact. Unknown target quality limits the confidence of this comparison; it
does not make the available candidate evidence irrelevant.

STOP and SELECT are distinct: neither a stopped epoch nor the best observed
proxy automatically selects a deployment model. Preserve a recoverable training
state separately from an inference-only artifact. Complete continuation needs
the relevant optimizer, component, normalization, schedule and RNG state, not
only model weights.

Evaluate stopping and selection separately after decisions freeze. A later
quality peak is a retrospective audit, not information available at the past
STOP. Finding an oracle winner does not prove that an online selector can find
it, and saving epochs does not excuse losing material useful performance.
