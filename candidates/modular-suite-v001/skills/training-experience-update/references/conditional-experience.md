# Conditional experience and its limits

Historical learning can nominate useful experiments without supplying a universal optimum. TNP transfers configuration/performance information and adapts to current observations; that is not a proof that raw feature distance predicts LR. Reflexion and ExpeL provide feedback-based learning examples, but wrong evaluators and inapplicable feedback can reinforce mistakes. A-Mem organizes memory; organization is not measured tuning quality.

Preserve observation versus intervention, independent versus overlapping samples, original visible facts versus hindsight, and actual versus proposed execution. Keep the strongest counterexample and the cost of acquiring the lesson. If a comparison is confounded, retain the association while withdrawing its causal claim.

Before loading more historical material, ask whether it can change the present choice. A short relevant case with an adverse response may be more useful than a large list of winning parameters. Retrieved similarity needs a task-specific explanation; a history database or embedding service is not required by this Skill.
